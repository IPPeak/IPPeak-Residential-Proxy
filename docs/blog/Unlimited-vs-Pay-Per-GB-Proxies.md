# Unlimited vs. Pay-Per-GB Proxies: Which Is More Cost-Effective?

When choosing a proxy service, the pricing model can have a significant impact on the overall cost of a project. Two common options are **Unlimited Proxies** and **Pay-Per-GB Proxies**.

At first glance, unlimited traffic may seem like the more cost-effective choice. However, that is not always the case. For projects with low traffic usage, paying by the gigabyte may be more economical. For high-volume tasks that run continuously, an unlimited plan can reduce the effective cost per gigabyte.

Therefore, the best way to determine which option offers better value is to consider **actual traffic usage and operating time**, rather than simply comparing package prices.

## What Are Pay-Per-GB Proxies?

Pay-Per-GB proxies charge based on the amount of network traffic actually used.

The calculation is straightforward:

**Proxy Cost = Traffic Usage × Price per GB**

For example, if a proxy plan costs $0.50/GB and a project consumes 20GB of traffic, the estimated cost would be:

**20GB × $0.50 = $10**

This pricing model separates proxy usage time from traffic costs. Users only pay for the traffic they actually consume, making it suitable for projects with relatively low traffic, short-term tasks, or predictable usage.

Common use cases include:

- Market research
- SEO analysis
- Website testing
- Small-scale data collection
- Price monitoring
- Short-term automation tasks

## What Are Unlimited Proxies?

Unlimited proxies are typically charged **by the hour or by the day**, rather than according to traffic consumption.

For example, if an unlimited proxy plan costs $10/hour, using the service for one hour would cost approximately $10. Within the provider's usage terms, large amounts of traffic during that period are not charged separately on a per-GB basis.

As a result, the effective cost per gigabyte decreases as traffic usage increases.

Suppose an unlimited proxy costs $10/hour:

With 2GB of traffic:

**$10 ÷ 2GB = $5/GB**

With 20GB of traffic:

**$10 ÷ 20GB = $0.50/GB**

Although the hourly price remains unchanged, the effective cost per GB becomes significantly lower as usage increases.

## How Do You Calculate the Break-Even Point?

To determine when an unlimited proxy becomes more cost-effective, you can calculate the **break-even point** between the two pricing models.

The formula is:

**Break-Even Traffic = Unlimited Price ÷ Pay-Per-GB Price**

For example:

- Unlimited proxy: $10/hour
- Pay-Per-GB proxy: $0.50/GB

Therefore:

**$10 ÷ $0.50 = 20GB**

Under these theoretical conditions, 20GB per hour is the cost break-even point.

If actual traffic is below 20GB/hour, the Pay-Per-GB option costs less. If traffic is significantly higher than 20GB/hour, the unlimited option may provide better cost efficiency.

This approach is more accurate than simply comparing hourly and per-GB prices because it takes actual usage into account.

## Traffic Is Not the Only Factor

Traffic volume is important, but it should not be the only consideration when evaluating proxy costs.

You also need to consider **usage duration and task patterns**.

For example, a project that runs for only 30 minutes per day may not benefit from a long-term unlimited plan, even if its traffic is relatively high during that short period.

On the other hand, if an application runs continuously for several hours and generates a large number of requests, Pay-Per-GB costs can increase rapidly.

Before selecting a pricing model, evaluate these three factors:

### Traffic Volume

Estimate how many gigabytes of traffic the project generates per hour or per day.

### Usage Duration

Determine how long the proxy needs to run each day and how long the project is expected to continue.

### Traffic Pattern

Consider whether traffic is generated continuously or concentrated during specific periods.

Combining these factors provides a more accurate estimate of the actual proxy cost.

## Comparing Actual Costs

Consider two different projects.

### Project A: Low-Traffic, Short-Term Task

A data collection task runs for 2 hours and uses a total of 6GB of traffic.

If the Pay-Per-GB price is $0.50/GB:

**6GB × $0.50 = $3**

With an unlimited proxy, the total cost would depend on the hourly rate.

For a relatively low-traffic task like this, Pay-Per-GB pricing generally makes it easier to control the budget.

### Project B: High-Traffic, Long-Running Task

Another project runs for 8 hours and generates a total of 240GB of traffic.

At $0.50/GB:

**240GB × $0.50 = $120**

If the unlimited proxy costs $10/hour:

**8 × $10 = $80**

In this example, the unlimited option saves $40 compared with Pay-Per-GB pricing.

This shows that the same proxy pricing model can produce very different cost results depending on the workload.

## How to Choose the Right Pricing Model

You can follow these steps to quickly evaluate your options:

**Step 1: Estimate Traffic**

Calculate the expected traffic usage per hour or per day.

**Step 2: Calculate Pay-Per-GB Cost**

Multiply the estimated traffic by the price per GB.

**Step 3: Calculate Unlimited Cost**

Multiply the hourly or daily price by the actual usage duration.

**Step 4: Compare Total Costs**

Compare the total expenses based on the actual project duration and workload.

If traffic patterns change over time, recalculate the costs accordingly. A project that initially benefits from Pay-Per-GB pricing may become more suitable for an unlimited plan as traffic increases.

## What Else Should You Consider When Choosing a Proxy?

Proxy pricing is not the only factor that matters.

When comparing different proxy services, also consider:

- Residential IP quality
- IP coverage
- IP rotation methods
- Sticky sessions
- HTTP and SOCKS5 support
- Concurrent connections
- Bandwidth and speed
- Session duration
- Traffic validity
- Usage policies

A cheaper proxy may not actually reduce your overall costs if it fails to provide the stability, geographic coverage, or connection capacity required by your project.

## Conclusion

**Pay-Per-GB proxies** are generally more suitable for low-traffic projects, flexible usage patterns, and tasks where users want to control costs based on actual consumption.

**Unlimited proxies** are better suited to high-traffic and continuously running tasks. Once actual traffic exceeds the cost break-even point between the two pricing models, an unlimited plan may provide better cost efficiency.

Therefore, instead of simply asking which proxy type is "cheaper," calculate your **traffic volume, usage duration, and total cost** first.

The key question is not:

**"Are unlimited proxies or Pay-Per-GB proxies cheaper?"**

It is:

**"Which pricing model provides the lowest total cost for my actual workload?"**

Using this approach can help reduce unnecessary proxy expenses while avoiding the purchase of more proxy capacity than your project actually needs.
